# Common RFE Triggers & Denial Grounds (Reference)

Used by the RFE-risk reviewer to pressure-test a package before filing. Organized
by category. For each risk, the reviewer should output: the issue, where it
appears, likelihood, and a concrete remediation.

## Cross-category
- **Conclusory assertions** without independent corroboration.
- **Boilerplate / templated recommendation letters** that read as one author.
- **Foreign-language exhibits** without certified translations.
- **Un-sourced statistics** or impact claims.
- **Mismatch** between the petition letter's claims and the actual exhibits.
- **Chronological gaps** or inconsistencies across CV, letters, and forms.

## EB-1A specific
- Criteria claimed but not actually met under the **plain language** (Step 1).
- Passing Step 1 but failing the **final merits** determination — evidence shows
  competence, not "top of the field / sustained acclaim."
- **Original contributions**: impact not shown *beyond the applicant's own work*
  (no adoption, citation context, licensing, or field influence).
- **Membership**: association admits by degree/dues, not outstanding achievement.
- **Judging**: routine internal job duty rather than selective external role.
- **Awards**: student/local/employer-internal rather than nationally recognized.
- **Authorship** treated as sufficient alone (it's one criterion, not the case).

## EB-1B specific
- Weak or missing **permanent job-offer** documentation.
- Private-employer path lacking proof of **3+ full-time researchers** and
  documented accomplishments.
- Evidence shows "published" but not "**internationally recognized as
  outstanding**."
- Fewer than 3 years qualifying experience, or experience not properly documented
  (employment letters with dates, duties, hours).

## EB-2 NIW specific (Dhanasar)
- **Endeavor** defined as a job title or too broadly; not a specific endeavor.
- **National importance** argued from geographic scope of work rather than the
  endeavor's broader field/industry/public implications.
- **Prong 2** evidence proves qualifications but not a record of *influence or
  progress* toward the endeavor.
- **Prong 3** balancing treated as a formality; no genuine argument why waiving
  PERM benefits the U.S.
- Citation counts with **no field-relative benchmark**.
- Missing **EB-2 base eligibility** proof (advanced degree evaluation or
  exceptional-ability evidence).

## Adjudication-trend flags (verify current USCIS posture at filing time)
- Increasing scrutiny of **AI-generated / templated** letters and petitions.
- Emphasis on **real-world adoption/impact** over raw citation volume.
- Close reading of whether "critical role" and "leading role" claims name a
  *distinguished* organization and the applicant's *specific* impact.

> The reviewer should treat this list as a checklist floor, not a ceiling, and
> always note that USCIS policy and adjudication trends shift — confirm current
> USCIS Policy Manual guidance at the time of filing.

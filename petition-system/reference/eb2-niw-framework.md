# EB-2 NIW — National Interest Waiver (Reference)

**Statute/Reg:** INA § 203(b)(2); waiver under § 203(b)(2)(B)(i)
**Form:** I-140 (**self-petition allowed**; no job offer, no PERM)
**Controlling framework:** *Matter of Dhanasar*, 26 I&N Dec. 884 (AAO 2016)

## Step 0 — EB-2 base eligibility (must qualify before the waiver)
The beneficiary must first qualify for EB-2 via **one** of:
- **Advanced degree** (U.S. master's or higher, or bachelor's + 5 years
  progressive experience in the specialty); **or**
- **Exceptional ability** in the sciences, arts, or business (meet ≥3 of the
  6 exceptional-ability criteria at 8 CFR § 204.5(k)(3)(ii)).

If base eligibility isn't met, the NIW analysis never begins.

## The Dhanasar three-prong test (all three required)

### Prong 1 — Substantial merit and national importance
The **proposed endeavor** has both substantial merit **and** national
importance.
- *Substantial merit* — can be in business, entrepreneurship, science,
  technology, culture, health, education, etc. Not limited to economics.
- *National importance* — focus on the **broader implications** of the
  endeavor, not the geographic scope of where the person works. Ask: does it
  have national or even global ramifications (a field, an industry, public
  health, national security, the economy)?
- **Define the endeavor specifically.** Vague ("I am a good software engineer")
  fails; specific ("develop X technique to improve Y for Z sector") succeeds.

### Prong 2 — Well positioned to advance the endeavor
The applicant is **well positioned** to advance the proposed endeavor. Factors:
- Education, skills, knowledge, and record of success in related efforts.
- A model or plan for future activities.
- Progress toward achieving the endeavor.
- Interest from potential customers, users, investors, or other relevant
  entities.
- Note: this is **not** a guarantee of success; it's about the person's
  trajectory and capacity.

### Prong 3 — Benefit to the U.S. to waive the requirements
On balance, it would be **beneficial to the United States** to waive the job
offer and (thus) the labor certification requirement. Factors:
- Impractical for the person to secure a job offer / labor certification.
- The U.S. would benefit from the contributions even absent a specific employer.
- The endeavor's urgency/importance outweighs the interest in protecting U.S.
  workers through the PERM process.

## Strategy notes
- The **endeavor**, not the résumé, is the center of gravity. Every piece of
  evidence should tie back to advancing the specifically defined endeavor.
- Prong 2 is where most evidence lives (publications, citations, patents,
  adoption, letters, funding, media). Prong 1 and Prong 3 are argued.
- Recommendation letters should speak to **national importance** and the
  person's **positioning**, not just praise credentials.
- Strong NIW cases increasingly need evidence of **real-world impact/adoption**,
  not only citation counts.

## Common failure modes (RFE / denial triggers)
- Endeavor defined too broadly or as a job title rather than an endeavor.
- National importance argued from job location rather than field-wide impact.
- Prong 2 evidence that shows competence but not a record of *influence*.
- Prong 3 treated as a formality with no genuine balancing argument.
- Citation counts presented without field-relative context (what's high in
  this field?).

# Evidence & Documentation Standards (Reference)

Applies across EB-1A, EB-1B, and EB-2 NIW. The team must hold every exhibit to
these standards.

## Burden of proof
- Standard is **"preponderance of the evidence"** — more likely than not (>50%).
  It is NOT "beyond a reasonable doubt." Frame arguments accordingly, but do not
  overstate; USCIS discounts conclusory claims.

## Independent, objective corroboration
Every material claim should be backed by evidence a skeptical adjudicator can
verify without taking anyone's word for it:
- Citation reports (Google Scholar, Web of Science, Scopus) — screenshot +
  date + field-relative context.
- Media coverage — publication name, circulation/reach, date, author, and a
  translation if not in English.
- Awards — the awarding body's criteria, selectivity, and reputation.
- Membership — the association's bylaws showing an achievement-based admission
  requirement.
- Salary — objective comparators (BLS/OES data, industry surveys) for the same
  occupation and locale.

## Translations
Any non-English document requires a **full English translation** plus the
translator's certification of competency and accuracy (8 CFR § 103.2(b)(3)).
Flag every foreign-language exhibit for translation.

## Recommendation / expert letters
- Mix **independent** experts (no prior relationship) with those who know the
  work directly; independent letters carry more weight.
- Each letter should be **specific** — describe the contribution and its
  impact, not generic praise. Identical/templated letters are a red flag.
- Include the recommender's credentials and basis for their expertise.
- Letters support but do not substitute for documentary evidence.

## Exhibit hygiene
- Number exhibits sequentially; maintain an exhibit index/table of contents.
- Every factual assertion in the petition letter cites a specific exhibit.
- Redact sensitive PII not needed for adjudication.
- Keep a source for every number — no un-sourced statistics.

## Absolute prohibitions (the QA/verifier agent enforces these)
- **Never fabricate** evidence, quotes, citations, letters, credentials, or
  facts. If evidence for a claim doesn't exist, flag the gap — do not invent it.
- **Never overstate** what a document shows.
- **Never** present AI-drafted recommendation letters as independently authored
  without the recommender's genuine review and adoption.
- Do not assert legal conclusions as guarantees of approval.

## Unauthorized practice of law (UPL) guardrail
This system **drafts and analyzes**; it does not replace a licensed attorney's
judgment. Every package must be reviewed and approved by a qualified U.S.
immigration attorney before filing. Outputs should carry a reviewer sign-off
line. Nothing here is legal advice to the end client.

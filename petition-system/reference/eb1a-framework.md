# EB-1A — Extraordinary Ability (Reference)

**Statute/Reg:** INA § 203(b)(1)(A); 8 CFR § 204.5(h)
**Form:** I-140 (no PERM labor certification, may self-petition)
**Standard:** Sustained national or international acclaim; among the small
percentage at the very top of the field.

## Two paths to qualify

### Path 1 — One-time major achievement
A single, major, internationally recognized award (e.g., Nobel Prize,
Academy Award, Olympic Medal). Rare; if genuinely applicable it largely
resolves the case.

### Path 2 — Meet at least 3 of 10 regulatory criteria
Per 8 CFR § 204.5(h)(3):

1. **Awards** — Nationally or internationally recognized prizes/awards for
   excellence in the field.
2. **Membership** — Membership in associations that require *outstanding
   achievements* of their members, judged by recognized experts.
3. **Published material about the person** — In professional or major trade
   publications or major media, relating to the person's work. Must include
   title, date, author.
4. **Judging** — Participation as a judge of the work of others (peer review,
   editorial board, grant/award panels, competition judging).
5. **Original contributions of major significance** — Original scientific,
   scholarly, artistic, athletic, or business-related contributions.
6. **Authorship** — Scholarly articles in professional journals or major media.
7. **Artistic exhibitions/showcases** — Display of work at artistic exhibitions.
8. **Leading or critical role** — For organizations with a distinguished
   reputation.
9. **High remuneration** — High salary or other significantly high pay relative
   to others in the field.
10. **Commercial success** — In the performing arts (box office, sales).

## The Kazarian two-step analysis (USCIS adjudication model)

**Step 1 — Antecedent procedural count.** Does the evidence satisfy the plain
language of at least 3 criteria? This is a counting exercise; do not import
"final merits" judgments here (per *Kazarian v. USCIS*, 596 F.3d 1115).

**Step 2 — Final merits determination.** Considering the evidence *in totality*,
does it establish sustained national/international acclaim and that the person
is among the small percentage at the very top? This is qualitative and is where
weak cases fail even after clearing Step 1.

## Strategy notes
- Aim to clearly satisfy **4+ criteria**, not the bare minimum of 3, to create
  cushion for adjudicator subjectivity.
- The strongest cases pair *breadth* (many criteria) with *depth* on
  Criterion 5 (original contributions of major significance) — the criterion
  most tied to the final-merits standard.
- "Major significance" requires impact **beyond** the person's own work: cited,
  adopted, licensed, implemented, or influencing the field. Evidence of impact
  (citations, adoption, downstream use, media, expert testimony) is essential.
- Comparable evidence is allowed only if a criterion "does not readily apply"
  to the occupation — argue this explicitly when used.

## Common failure modes (design RFE-risk review around these)
- Treating publications/citations as automatically satisfying "original
  contributions of major significance."
- Membership in associations that admit by dues/degree rather than achievement.
- Awards that are student, local, or internal-to-employer.
- Judging that is a routine job duty vs. selective external invitation.
- No independent corroboration — over-reliance on the petitioner's own say-so.

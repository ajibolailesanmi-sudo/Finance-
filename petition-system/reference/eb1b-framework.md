# EB-1B — Outstanding Professor or Researcher (Reference)

**Statute/Reg:** INA § 203(b)(1)(B); 8 CFR § 204.5(i)
**Form:** I-140 (no PERM; **employer must petition** — no self-petition)
**Standard:** Internationally recognized as outstanding in a specific academic
area.

## Threshold requirements (all three must be met)
1. **International recognition** as outstanding in the academic field.
2. **At least 3 years** of experience in teaching and/or research in the field.
3. **Qualifying offer** from the petitioning employer:
   - Tenured or tenure-track teaching position; **or**
   - Comparable permanent research position at a university/institution of
     higher education; **or**
   - Comparable research position with a private employer that employs at least
     3 full-time researchers **and** has documented accomplishments in the field.

## Meet at least 2 of 6 criteria — 8 CFR § 204.5(i)(3)(i)
1. **Major prizes/awards** for outstanding achievement.
2. **Membership** in associations requiring outstanding achievements.
3. **Published material** in professional publications *written by others* about
   the person's work (must include title, date, author).
4. **Judging** the work of others in the same or allied field.
5. **Original scientific or scholarly research contributions** to the field.
6. **Authorship** of scholarly books or articles (in journals with
   international circulation).

## How EB-1B differs from EB-1A (get this right)
- **Employer-sponsored**, not self-petition. There must be a permanent job offer.
- Standard is "**internationally recognized as outstanding**," a notch below
  EB-1A's "top of the field / sustained acclaim," but still high.
- Only **2 of 6** criteria required (vs. 3 of 10).
- No final-merits language identical to EB-1A, but USCIS still applies a
  totality analysis to the "internationally recognized" threshold.

## Strategy notes
- Often the strongest option for university faculty and permanent-position
  research scientists who may not yet meet EB-1A's higher bar.
- Criterion 5 (original contributions) + Criterion 6 (authorship) are the
  backbone for most academic researchers; add judging (peer review) and
  published material about the work for cushion.
- The employer offer letter and evidence of the institution's standing are
  as important as the beneficiary's evidence — do not neglect them.

## Common failure modes
- Missing or defective permanent-offer documentation.
- Private-employer path without proof of 3+ full-time researchers and
  documented accomplishments.
- Conflating "internationally recognized" with merely "published."

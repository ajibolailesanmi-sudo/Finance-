## 1. Bottom line up front

- **Primary recommendation: EB-2 National Interest Waiver (Dhanasar).** It fits
  the client's self-petition intent, does not require the "top of the field"
  final-merits finding, and her single strongest asset — real-world adoption of

  **plausible-to-strong** case once corroboration is gathered.
- **Backup / parallel: EB-1A.** She can likely clear the Kazarian **Step 1** count
  (3, targeting 4, criteria), but **Step 2 final merits** ("sustained national or
  international acclaim … among the small percentage at the very top") is a
  **genuine risk**. As of today EB-1A is a *stretch* case, not a comfortable one.
- **EB-1B: not viable.** No permanent/tenure-track or qualifying research offer,
  and it is structurally incompatible with self-petition intent. Excluded from
  further analysis (correctly flagged at intake).
- **Hedging option to raise with the attorney:** because the two categories sit in
  **different India queues** and an I-140 can be filed in each, concurrently
  filing **EB-1A and EB-2 NIW** is worth pricing out — NIW as the reliable floor,
  EB-1A as the faster-queue upside. Decision depends on current bulletin movement
  and budget; not a strategist call to make unilaterally.

## 3. EB-2 NIW analysis — Matter of Dhanasar (PRIMARY)

**Analytical model:** three-prong Dhanasar test; all three prongs required;
preponderance-of-the-evidence standard. The **endeavor**, not the résumé, is the
center of gravity.

### 3.1 Defining the endeavor (do this precisely — most NIW failures start here)

Recommended framing (tighter than the CV's version, which drifts toward a job
description):

> *"Develop, validate, and deploy machine-learning flood-forecasting systems —
> centered on the open-source FloodNet model and sensor-fusion methods — that give
> U.S. cities earlier and more accurate urban flood warnings, and drive their
> operational adoption by municipal water and emergency-management authorities."*

This keeps a **specific technical core** (ML flood forecasting / sensor fusion)
plus a **specific deployment pathway** (municipal operational adoption), which is
where her evidence actually is. Avoid the generic "I am a good ML scientist"
framing that triggers RFEs.

### 3.2 Prong strength matrix

| Prong | Evidence on hand (per CV) | Strength | What would make it Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Prong 1 — Substantial merit & national importance** | Flooding is the costliest U.S. natural-disaster category; endeavor targets public safety, critical infrastructure, and economic loss reduction nationwide; ML flood prediction is a recognized climate-resilience priority. | **Strong** | Objective third-party sourcing for the "costliest disaster" and national-scope claims (NOAA/NWS, FEMA, congressional or agency reports); tie the *specific* endeavor (not just "climate AI") to a documented national priority. Merit is easy; nail national importance with cited sources, not adjectives. |
| **Prong 2 — Well positioned to advance the endeavor** | 19 papers (NeurIPS/ICML/Water Resources Research/Nature Climate Change); 1,240 citations, h-17, top paper 310; **FloodNet adopted by Boston Water & Sewer + Chennai pilot + NGO early-warning tool**; 2,300 GitHub stars; pending sensor-fusion patent; NSF co-PI ($400k); MIT Tech Review mention; technical lead of municipal-partnerships program. | **Plausible → Strong** | This is the case's engine. Convert claims to exhibits: **adoption letters** from Boston Water & Sewer, the Chennai pilot entity, and the NGO describing operational use and impact; dated citation report with **field-relative** benchmarking; NSF award notice specifying her co-PI contribution; USPTO filing receipt; GitHub metrics screenshot. Letters must speak to *influence and trajectory*, not credentials. |
| **Prong 3 — On balance, beneficial to waive job offer/PERM** | Self-petitioner; endeavor benefits the U.S. across many jurisdictions, not tied to one employer; urgent public-safety/climate rationale. | **Plausible → Strong** | Articulate the genuine balancing argument: her impact is *cross-jurisdictional* (multiple cities/agencies) and open-source, so tying her to a single PERM-sponsoring employer would impede national benefit; PERM's labor-market-protection purpose is weakly served here. Do not treat Prong 3 as a formality — write the actual balance. |

### 3.3 NIW read

Once corroboration is assembled, this is a **solid NIW** — arguably the client's
best-fit path. The differentiator versus a run-of-the-mill "competent researcher"
NIW is the **documented operational adoption** of her work by public
infrastructure entities. That is exactly the "real-world impact/adoption" that
current NIW adjudication increasingly wants to see (framework §"Strategy notes").
The main risk is not the legal standard but **evidentiary**: today it is all
assertion. The case rises or falls on getting the adoption letters and a
field-contextualized citation record.

## 4. EB-1A analysis — Kazarian two-step (BACKUP / PARALLEL)

**Analytical model:** *Kazarian v. USCIS*. **Step 1** — plain-language count of
criteria met (pure counting; no merits judgment imported). **Step 2** — totality
"final merits" determination of sustained acclaim / very top of field. A case can
pass Step 1 and still fail Step 2. Score all 10 criteria:

| # | Criterion (8 CFR 204.5(h)(3)) | Evidence on hand | Strength | What would make it Strong |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Awards** | ICML Climate Change AI **Workshop** Best Paper (2021); Rackham **Predoctoral** Fellowship (2019) | **Weak** | Both are vulnerable: workshop-level best paper (not the main-conference award) with unproven selectivity, and a **student/internal** fellowship — USCIS routinely discounts student awards. Would need award selectivity data + evidence of national/international recognition; even then, marginal. Likely not counted. |
| 2 | **Membership** | IEEE (regular), ACM (regular) | **None** | Regular IEEE/ACM grades are dues/degree-based, not achievement-juried — fails the plain language. Only fixable if she qualifies for/obtains **IEEE Senior Member** or **Fellow** (juried) grade. Confirm; otherwise drop this criterion. |
| 3 | **Published material about her** | Quoted (named) in MIT Technology Review (2023); HydroAI blog | **Weak → Plausible** | MIT Tech Review is major media, but the piece appears to be *about the field*, quoting her — the criterion wants material **about her and her work**. HydroAI blog is employer-controlled → excluded. Needs 1–2 more pieces genuinely centered on her/FloodNet (with title, date, author, reach) to count. |
| 4 | **Judging** | Reviewer NeurIPS (2021–24), ICML (2022–24), Water Resources Research; program committee, CCAI Workshop 2023 | **Strong** | Already the cleanest criterion. Just needs documentary proof: reviewer invitation/confirmation records and program-committee listing. Selective external peer review at top venues satisfies this well. |
| 5 | **Original contributions of major significance** | FloodNet adopted by 2 municipal water authorities + NGO; 2,300 GitHub stars; sensor-fusion patent; 1,240 citations | **Plausible → Strong** | The pivotal criterion (most tied to final merits). "Major significance" needs impact **beyond her own work** — the adoption letters supply exactly that. With strong Boston/Chennai/NGO letters + field-relative citation context + independent expert testimony on FloodNet's influence, this can reach Strong. Citations/patent *alone* do not establish "major significance." |
| 6 | **Authorship** | 19 peer-reviewed papers at top venues | **Strong** | Well met on plain language. Provide the full publication list with venues and author order. |
| 7 | **Artistic exhibitions** | N/A | **None** | Not applicable to the field; do not force it. |
| 8 | **Leading / critical role** | Technical lead, HydroAI Labs municipal-partnerships program (team of 6) | **Plausible** | Role is plausible, but the criterion requires the **organization** to have a *distinguished reputation*. HydroAI Labs is described only as "a private research org" — its standing is unproven. Needs evidence of HydroAI's reputation (funding, notable clients, press) plus a letter defining her role as leading/critical. Absent that, weak. |
| 9 | **High remuneration** | $195k/yr | **Weak → Plausible** | No comparator. For a Boston-area senior ML research scientist, $195k is respectable but not obviously *significantly high* relative to the field — ML pay is high. Needs BLS/OES or industry-survey data for the same occupation/locale; may show she is above median but not clearly at the top percentile. Handle with care; could backfire if comparators show she is merely typical. |
| 10 | **Commercial success (performing arts)** | N/A | **None** | Not applicable. |

### 4.1 Step 1 — count

**Clearly countable: #4 Judging (Strong), #6 Authorship (Strong).** The third
solid criterion is **#5 Original contributions**, which is *plausible now and
convertible to Strong* with the adoption letters. That gets her to the **3-criteria
minimum**. Criteria #3 (published material) and #8 (leading role) are the realistic
candidates for a **4th** with targeted evidence-building. Meeting the framework's
"target 4+" cushion is achievable but **not yet in hand**.

### 4.2 Step 2 — final merits (the real risk)

Even assuming Step 1 is cleared, the totality question is harder. Anjali is a
strong **mid-career** research scientist (8 years, h-17). Publications and peer
review are common to *many* competent researchers and, on their own, do not
demonstrate "sustained national or international acclaim … among the small
percentage at the very top." **The only element that meaningfully lifts her toward
that standard is FloodNet's operational adoption** — and that must be
independently documented and framed as field-shaping to carry the merits burden.

**Candid read:** EB-1A is a **stretch** today. It is defensible to file only if
(a) the adoption story is fully corroborated and framed as major significance, and
(b) at least a 4th criterion is solidified. Filing EB-1A on the current record —
assertions only — would risk an RFE on final merits and, plausibly, a denial. Do
not oversell it to the client as a safe path.

## 6. Final-merits / totality risk read

- **NIW:** Low legal-standard risk; **moderate evidentiary risk** driven entirely
  by the fact that nothing is corroborated yet. Prong 1 and Prong 3 are argued and
  winnable; Prong 2 is strong *on paper* but currently unproven. Priority: adoption
  letters + field-relative citation context.
- **EB-1A:** **Moderate-to-high final-merits risk.** Step 1 is reachable; Step 2 is
  where this case is exposed. The record reads as "excellent researcher," and the
  regulation demands "one of the very top." Only the FloodNet adoption narrative
  bridges that gap, and it must be independently and vividly documented.
- **Cross-cutting risk:** the single biggest issue for *both* paths is that the
  entire file is presently **uncorroborated assertion** (intake §4). Documentation,
  not eligibility theory, is the critical path.

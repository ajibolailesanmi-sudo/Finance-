Dear Sir or Madam:

This letter supports the self-petition of Dr. Anjali Rao for classification as a
member of the professions holding an advanced degree, together with a waiver of
the job-offer and labor-certification requirements in the national interest under
INA § 203(b)(2)(B)(i). The governing standard is *Matter of Dhanasar*, 26 I&N
Dec. 884 (AAO 2016), and the applicable burden of proof is **preponderance of
the evidence** — that is, whether it is *more likely than not* that each element
is satisfied. *Matter of Chawathe*, 25 I&N Dec. 369, 375–76 (AAO 2010). We do not
contend, and USCIS does not require, proof beyond a reasonable doubt.

## III. Prong 1 — Substantial merit and national importance

**Governing standard (Dhanasar Prong 1):** the petitioner must demonstrate that
"the foreign national's proposed endeavor has both substantial merit and national
importance." *Dhanasar*, 26 I&N Dec. at 889. National importance turns on the
**broader implications** of the endeavor, not the geographic location where the
work is performed. *Id.* at 889.

**Substantial merit.** The merit of the endeavor is not seriously contestable.
Reducing the loss of life, property, and critical-infrastructure damage caused by
urban flooding, through more accurate and earlier machine-learning forecasting,
plainly has substantial merit in the areas of public safety, science and
technology, and the economy — all fields *Dhanasar* expressly recognizes.
*Dhanasar*, 26 I&N Dec. at 889.

**National importance.** The national importance of the endeavor rests on the
scale of the flooding problem it addresses and on the field-wide, cross-
jurisdictional character of the solution. We are careful here not to argue
national importance from Dr. Rao's job location; the argument is about the
endeavor's implications for U.S. public safety and infrastructure nationally.

The petition's factual premise for national importance is that flooding is the
costliest category of natural disaster in the United States. On the current
record, that premise is asserted in the CV (Exhibit 1) **without an objective
source**, and it must not appear in the filed letter uncorroborated.

> `[GAP — needs: Exhibit 24 — an objective governmental source (e.g., NOAA/NWS,
> FEMA, or a congressional/agency report) establishing flooding as a top-tier
> national economic and safety priority. This is the single most conspicuous
> unsourced statistic in the file (evidence map §5). It is public data and
> highly obtainable. Until obtained, the "costliest natural disaster" statement
> is argued, not evidenced.]`

The endeavor's national reach is further shown by the fact that FloodNet is not a
single-city tool but is used across multiple, independent jurisdictions and by a
non-governmental early-warning program — evidence that the endeavor's benefits
are diffuse and national (indeed international) rather than local. That premise
depends on the adoption letters identified under Prong 2 (Exhibits 6, 7, 8),
which do not yet exist:

> `[GAP — needs: Exhibits 6, 7, 8 — the FloodNet adoption letters (Boston Water &
> Sewer Commission; Chennai pilot entity; NGO early-warning program). These
> supply the "multiple jurisdictions" premise on which the national-importance
> argument partly rests. See Prong 2.]`

Independent expert testimony explaining the field-wide implications of Dr. Rao's
approach would also support this prong.

> `[GAP — needs: Exhibit 10 (Dr. Elena Vasquez, Stanford — independent) and
> Exhibit 12 (1–2 additional independent experts, not yet identified),
> addressing the broader/national implications of ML flood forecasting and of
> Dr. Rao's specific methods. Letters must speak to field-wide importance, not
> generic praise.]`

The MIT Technology Review coverage (2023) may provide secondary support for the
national salience of the field.

> `[GAP — needs: Exhibit 16 — full copy of the MIT Technology Review article with

**Assessment.** The legal theory of Prong 1 is sound, and substantial merit is

## IV. Prong 2 — Well positioned to advance the endeavor

**Governing standard (Dhanasar Prong 2):** the petitioner must show she is "well
positioned to advance the proposed endeavor." *Dhanasar*, 26 I&N Dec. at 890.
Relevant factors include the individual's education, skills, knowledge, and
**record of success** in related efforts; a model or plan for future activities;
any progress toward achieving the endeavor; and the interest of potential
customers, users, or other relevant entities. *Id.* This prong does not require a
guarantee of ultimate success; it asks about capacity and trajectory. *Id.*

This prong is the center of gravity for the petition. It is also the prong most
exposed on the current record: **as of today, not one of the corroborating
exhibits below exists in the file.** We set out the argument and mark each
supporting exhibit as a gap so that no assertion outruns the evidence.

**(a) Record of success — publications and citations.** Dr. Rao reports 19
peer-reviewed papers at leading venues (including NeurIPS, ICML, Water Resources
Research, and a Nature Climate Change co-authorship), approximately 1,240
citations, an h-index of 17, and a most-cited paper at 310 citations (Exhibit 1).
These figures are presently sourced only to the CV and lack a dated, verifiable
report and — critically — field-relative context. Bare citation counts without
context ("what is high in ML-for-climate?") are a recognized RFE trigger.

> `[GAP — needs: Exhibit 4 — dated Google Scholar/Scopus/Web of Science citation

## V. Prong 3 — On balance, beneficial to waive the job-offer/labor-certification requirement

**Governing standard (Dhanasar Prong 3):** the petitioner must show that, "on
balance, it would be beneficial to the United States to waive the requirements of
a job offer and thus of a labor certification." *Dhanasar*, 26 I&N Dec. at 890.
Relevant considerations include whether, in light of the endeavor's merit and
national importance, it would be impractical either for the individual to secure
a job offer or for the labor-certification process to serve its purpose here; and
whether the United States would benefit from the individual's contributions even
if other qualified U.S. workers are available. *Id.* at 890–91. This prong is not
a formality; it requires a genuine balancing argument.

Three features of this case support waiver on balance:

1. **Cross-jurisdictional, not employer-specific, benefit.** The value of
   Dr. Rao's endeavor flows to multiple independent public entities and to the
   public through an open-source model — not to a single employer. Tying her to a
   specific PERM-sponsoring employer would tend to narrow work whose benefit is,
   by design, diffuse across many U.S. cities and agencies. This premise depends
   on the multi-entity adoption facts under Prong 2.

   > `[GAP — needs: Exhibits 6, 7, 8 (and 13) — the adoption letters that
   > substantiate the "cross-jurisdictional, open-source, not tied to one
   > employer" premise on which this balancing argument rests. Without them the
   > argument is asserted, not grounded.]`

2. **Self-petitioning / open-source posture.** Dr. Rao petitions on her own
   behalf, and the endeavor's open-source character means its benefits are not
   contingent on any one hiring decision. The labor-market-protection purpose of
   PERM — testing whether a qualified U.S. worker is available for a specific job
   — is weakly served where the contribution is a publicly available model
   adopted across jurisdictions rather than a single position.

3. **Urgency and importance of the endeavor.** To the extent Prong 1's
   national-importance showing is established (pending Exhibit 24 and the
   independent letters), the public-safety and infrastructure stakes of earlier,
   more accurate flood warning weigh in favor of waiver.

**Assessment.** Prong 3 is the prong least dependent on documentary exhibits and
most dependent on the quality of the balancing argument itself. The argument here
is genuine rather than pro forma, and it can be strong on its own terms — provided
the multi-jurisdiction premise it borrows from Prong 2 (Exhibits 6–8) is
substantiated. No separate Prong 3 exhibit is required beyond what Prong 2 already
needs.
